Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Pleasanton

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

On every Pleasanton job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • An overflow caused by an overloaded drum or by using too much detergent.
  • Dishwasher parts that wear out, including the door gasket, the drain hose and the seal on the pump.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Kitchen Cabinets

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. Swelling and separation follow, and for weeks the soaked cabinet boxes may trap moisture against the wall.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Mold Behind Appliances

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.
  • Which appliance leaked and whether it was a fast flood or a slow seep.
  • Whether mold has started and has to be removed before the area can be dried.
  • How long the leak ran before it was found, especially with a slow drip behind an appliance.
