Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. When it happens in Pleasanton, Pleasanton Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
