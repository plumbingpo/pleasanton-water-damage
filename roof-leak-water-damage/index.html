Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in Pleasanton

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. Inside the home, our team takes out saturated insulation, dries the wood and drywall, treats any mold and repairs the ceiling. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

Your First Steps

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Contain the WaterPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • Fasteners working loose over time, so water follows the nail shank into the attic.
  • Skylight seals and curbs that dry out, crack and let rain run inside.
  • Shingles that are cracked, curled or blown off by wind, exposing the underlayment.
  • Gaps at chimneys, vents and sidewalls where the flashing has separated or rusted through.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Wall Cavities

Rain sometimes follows the framing into a wall, where the insulation and the hidden side of the drywall take it up. You might notice nothing more than a peeling patch or a puffy baseboard.

Hidden Attic Mold

A dark, warm attic with damp wood and paper-faced drywall suits mold well. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.

Ceiling Drywall

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. Joints open, tape peels, and a waterlogged piece may fall without much notice.

Lights and Wiring

Recessed cans, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are low points where roof water collects and drips through. Wet connections can short or corrode, so the circuit should stay off until it is checked.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. A leak put down to an old, worn or neglected roof is usually denied, and fixing the roof may be treated separately from the interior work. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

What Drives the Price

  • How far down the leak traveled, from the attic to the walls and floor.
