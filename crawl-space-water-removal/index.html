Crawl Space Water Removal: What Pleasanton Homeowners Should Know

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • Stains on the support piers and stem walls that show how high water stood
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
What We Do

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Contaminated Water

Water under a house is often not clean. A leaking drain line or groundwater carrying soil and waste is Category 2 or 3 and needs protective gear and disinfection.

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.

Going in while water is pooled is a risk we advise against. Electrical cables and boxes are common down there, and wet ones can energize the water. Pumping out the water usually takes a few hours. Drying the framing takes longer, typically 3 to 5 days with a dehumidifier and air movers running under the house. It can run longer when the beams are large, the ground is uncovered, or the flooding went unnoticed for weeks. We go by moisture readings, not by the calendar.

Some wet soil following a big storm happens often, yet water that pools is a problem and needs attention. Even an inch or two keeps the humidity high enough to rot wood and grow mold.
