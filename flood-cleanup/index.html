Whether the water came from a storm, an overflowing river, a backed-up sewer, a failed sump pump or a burst plumbing line, flood damage needs a trained crew right away. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. Pleasanton Restoration handles emergency flood cleanup across Pleasanton and answers 24/7 to pump out the water, sanitize what it touched and restore your property safely.

We start with fast water extraction, using truck-mounted and submersible pumps that can move thousands of gallons per hour. What a Professional Brings to Flood Cleanup

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Improper cleanup also tends to miss moisture you cannot see, and within days that trapped water can cause a major mold problem. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Standing water anywhere on your property, whatever the source
  • Stains along baseboards and lower walls that mark the flood level
  • An overflowing washing machine, dishwasher or water heater
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
  • Storm damage that lets rain in through the roof or walls

How to Prevent Flood Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
  • Extend your downspouts to carry rain from the roof out past the foundation
  • Regrade low spots so soil around the whole foundation falls away from the walls
  • Install backwater valves on sewer lines to stop sewage from backing up into your home
  • Debris in a gutter or storm drain blocks proper flow, so clean them out regularly
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
