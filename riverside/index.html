Restoration Challenges Riverside Homeowners Face

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. Our crews in Riverside follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: A tub left filling, a sink with a blocked overflow hole or a toilet that runs over can soak a bathroom floor quickly. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. If the toilet bowl was the source, assume the water is unsanitary and have the area disinfected.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. You might see trim pulling away, a discolored spot spreading or drywall that gives when pressed. On these calls in Riverside, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. We pump out the spill and test the surrounding floor and wall bases, since water tends to run under them.

Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear. Stop using water in the house and keep people and pets away until a trained crew arrives in Riverside.
